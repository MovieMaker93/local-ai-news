Framework opened pre-orders on September 30 for a 192GB LPDDR5X-8533 configuration of the Framework Desktop built on the AMD Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 495+ — 16 Zen 5 cores, Radeon 8065S graphics and 273 GB/s memory bandwidth — and the first batch sold through within hours. NVIDIA open-sources OpenShell, a kernel-level sandbox runtime for autonomous agents

OpenShell, part of NVIDIA's Open Agent Safety Platform and now trending on GitHub, is an Apache 2.0 Rust runtime that runs coding agents (OpenClaw, Claude Code, Codex) inside kernel-level sandboxes governed by declarative policy — sandboxed execution, controlled service access, credential management and formal policy analysis. It extends to third-party Arm/Intel CPUs and pairs with 'Sentry', a monitoring reference design on a separate BlueField-4 DPU.

QATFactory: deployment-aligned quantization-aware training and distillation for LLMs

An open-source framework for quantization-aware distillation (QAD) and quantization-aware RL (QARL) that simulates deployment-time quantization during training, targeting the accuracy collapse seen when aggressive post-training quantization is applied to low-precision inference.

Expert-parallel communication across PCIe-connected consumer GPUs

Targets MoE inference with experts split across cheap PCIe-connected consumer GPUs, cutting the inter-GPU communication overhead that grows with experts-activated-per-token — the regime where multi-consumer-GPU MoE serving either works or stalls.

arXiv · 2026-09-30

RATIO: quantization on reasoning models makes them overthink, not just err

Shows that post-training quantization on reasoning models not only degrades accuracy but also lengthens reasoning trajectories (overthinking), and proposes token-level inference optimization to recover both quality and efficiency in quantized reasoners.

arXiv · 2026-09-30

Low-discrepancy dither curbs error build-up in quantized recurrent-state caches

For low-precision Mamba/hybrid recurrent-state caches — where rounding errors feed back into every later update — proposes a low-discrepancy dither rounding scheme that beats production-style stochastic rounding over long generations.

Smaller models write better rejects: preference-distillation scaling across 7B-72B

Across students from 7B to 72B, smaller frozen models generate better rejected examples for preference distillation than the student's own outputs or larger teachers, cutting distillation data-generation cost while improving student quality.
